\section{Motivation and Context}\label{sec:introduction/motivation_and_context}

It is commonly observed that learning to program can be overwhelming and difficult, and by extension is difficult to teach. Programming is often abstract and unintuitive to new programmers. Many people, especially students learning it for the first time, are not experienced in thinking algorithmically or computational thinking. This is not helped by the lack of intrinsic direction when writing a script. This is before considering other challenges associated with text-based languages such as syntax.

One approach to solve this problem is to make use of video games to compartmentalise the challenges of learning and reducing the intimidation of learning the skills and logic for programming. These often fall into a few broad categories which will be defined and described in detail within \autoref{sec:background/related}. These categories are the \textquote{\textit{Sandbox Program}} with little-to-no intrinsic  direction, the \textquote{\textit{Instruction Sequence Game}} with puzzles designed around programming and the \textquote{\textit{Incidental System}} where analogies to software development are less direct, and potentially unintentional.

The \citet{WelshGov-highStandardsAnd} has emphasises on \textquote{[b]reaking down barriers} for \textquote{all ages, at all ages} for education in many areas include digital competence in objective 2 of a policy and strategy document regarding education. For adult learning especially they put emphasis on online tools. In a increasingly digital world, new, more engaging approaches are required to increase access to learning programming.

\section{Aims and Objectives}\label{sec:introduction/aims_and_objectives}

The aim of this project was to evaluate the efficacy of using video-games to teach programming through its games mechanics whilst avoiding the aforementioned ‘instruction sequence’ paradigm, hoping to find a middle ground between the three approaches. This was be done by simulating a short development period to make a platforming game and then evaluating the results.

\textbf{The project had the following objectives:}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Objective \arabic*:}, leftmargin=*]\label{objectives}
    \item Research the Welsh curriculum (and English if required as a backup) and design the progression of the game around it, incorporating required programming techniques such as iteration, into its mechanics.\label{objectives/01}
    \item Attempt to design and program a game that incorporates programming into its mechanics and core game-loop, that avoids the ‘instruction sequence’ paradigm.\label{objectives/02}
    \item Evaluate the plausibility of designing said game within the tight time-frame presented by this project.\label{objectives/03}
    \item If possible, evaluate the game to measure how approachable and enjoyable users find the experience using user testing and surveys, accounting for experience with both video games and programming.\label{objectives/04}
\end{enumerate}
